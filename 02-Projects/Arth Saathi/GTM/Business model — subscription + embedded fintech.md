# Business model — subscription + embedded fintech

> The wedge is HR. The valuation engine is fintech.

## Revenue layers (mature stack)

| # | Layer | Take rate | Margin | Live |
|---|------|-----------|--------|------|
| 1 | **Subscription** | ARPU ₹400/mo blended | 80% GM | Now |
| 2 | **UPI salary disbursement** | 0.3–0.5% per ₹ | 80%+ (Razorpay cost 0.05%) | Q1 |
| 3 | **Earned Wage Access (EWA)** | 0–4% per advance (~₹100 on ₹5K) | High | Q3 |
| 4 | **Employee micro-loans** | 2–5% referral share (KreditBee, FlexiLoans) | Referral | Q3 |
| 5 | **Group insurance** | 7.5–15% broker commission | Broker | Q3 |
| 6 | **Aadhaar eKYC** | ₹15/verification | High | Q1 |

## Why this composition works (comp evidence)
- **Shopify** — 76% revenue from Merchant Solutions, not subscription
- **Toast** — ~75% revenue from payments at 48bps
- **Deel** — 25–30% from embedded fintech
- **BharatPe** — 60% fintech (₹1,000 Cr of ₹1,734 Cr FY25)

Pattern: **Vertical SMB-OS players globally earn 70–80% of mature revenue from embedded financial services, not subscription.**

## 18-month unit economics target
- 10K shops
- ₹6 Cr subscription ARR + ₹2 Cr fintech take-rate
- 82% GM
- K-factor 25 (viral coefficient from employee app)
- D30 retention ≥80%
- CAC <₹2,500
- Payback <18 months

## Why subscription alone wouldn't be enough
Shop owner can't pay ₹6,999/mo (Keka pricing). At ₹500/mo blended ARPU, you need fintech rails to hit venture-scale ARR. Khatabook had 10M MAU and couldn't crack monetization — they had distribution without rails.
