# Vertical SMB-OS earns most revenue from embedded fintech

> The wedge is workflow software. The valuation engine is the financial-services rail underneath.

## Context
Across mature vertical SMB software companies, **70–80% of revenue at scale comes from embedded financial services**, not subscription:

| Company | Vertical | Fintech revenue share |
|---------|----------|----------------------|
| **Shopify** | E-commerce | 76% (Merchant Solutions) |
| **Toast** | Restaurants | ~75% (payments @ 48bps) |
| **Deel** | Global hiring | 25–30% (and growing) |
| **BharatPe** | Indian merchants | 60% (₹1,000 Cr of ₹1,734 Cr FY25) |

The subscription line never gets you to venture-scale ARR alone. You use it to **buy the right to attach financial rails** on top of cleanly captured operational data.

## Why this is structurally true
Workflow software collects operational data → operational data underwrites financial decisions → financial products are higher take-rate and stickier than subscription.

- Shop sells products → you process payments
- Shop hires people → you disburse salary via UPI
- Shop pays salary → you offer EWA to employees
- Shop runs payroll → you underwrite credit for employees
- Shop has 12 months of clean data → you underwrite SMB working capital

## Implication
A B2B SaaS pitch that pencils only on subscription ARR is leaving the valuation engine on the table. The order is:

1. Win the daily-habit workflow (subscription as proof of stickiness)
2. Attach a payment rail (low-margin, high-volume)
3. Attach a lending rail (high-margin, sticks even harder)
4. Attach insurance / commerce / referral fees

## Implication for category choice
Pick a vertical where the workflow data naturally feeds financial decisions. HR/payroll → salary disbursement, EWA, employee loans, group insurance. Inventory → invoice financing. Bookings → BNPL.
