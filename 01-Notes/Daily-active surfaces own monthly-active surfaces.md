# Daily-active surfaces own monthly-active surfaces

> The product the user opens every day wins the customer for every other product they need.

## Context
In B2B SMB:
- **Khatabook** is monthly (ledger reconciliation)
- **Vyapar** is monthly (billing cycle)
- **Tally** is quarterly (filings)
- **Attendance** is daily

The product that owns the 9 AM "who's coming today?" question owns the customer for everything downstream — payroll, billing, lending, insurance. **Distribution flows from the daily habit upward, never the reverse.**

## Why
Habit formation requires repetition. A user who opens your app once a month never builds muscle memory; they have to be re-acquired every month via paid ads. A user who opens your app daily becomes the *default* place to do all related tasks.

This is why:
- Notion (daily) ate Confluence's (weekly) lunch
- Slack (real-time) ate email's slot for internal comms
- Khatabook (monthly) couldn't ride its 10M MAU into payroll

## Implication for product sequencing
Build the daily-habit product first, even if monetization is downstream. Sequencing matters more than feature scope.

**Wrong sequence:** ledger → staff (Khatabook's Pagarkhata path, killed 2025)
**Right sequence:** attendance → payroll → billing → fintech (Arth Saathi's path)

## Generalization
Ask: what's the daily-frequency question my user asks? Build the answer to that question first; everything else compounds on top.
